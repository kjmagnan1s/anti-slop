What most people get wrong about hiring is that they treat it like a checklist.

Last quarter I interviewed forty candidates for two roles, and the pattern was impossible to miss. The best candidates weren't the ones with the longest resumes. They were the ones who asked the hardest questions.

Hiring isn't about credentials. It's about curiosity.

One candidate spent her entire interview asking about our failed projects. Another asked why our onboarding took six weeks. Those two got the offers.

The lesson: curiosity compounds.

Curiosity. That's it. That's the whole filter. Skills and pedigree stop mattering the moment a person stops asking questions, and that's the real game-changer. We've rebuilt our entire process to leverage it.

In the end, hiring was never about the candidates. It was about us.
