When the city library opened its makerspace in 2019, it marked a pivotal moment in the evolution of public learning. What began as a single 3D printer has become a testament to the community's appetite for hands-on education — and the numbers back it up.

Students delve into robotics kits after school. Hobbyists tinker with laser cutters on weekends. Makers reserve the sewing machines weeks in advance, and creators — from retirees to teenagers — fill every open workshop. The programming spans everything from beginner soldering to advanced fabrication.

The makerspace has reshaped the learning landscape of the entire county. Staff say demand keeps growing — last month's waitlist hit 300 names.

Whatever comes next, one thing is clear: the future looks bright for spaces like this, and only time will tell how far the model can spread.
