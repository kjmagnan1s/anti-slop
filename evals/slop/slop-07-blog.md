Nobody designed the modern commute. People tend to accept it the way they accept weather, something that happens to them rather than something they chose.

What makes this hard is that the commute grew one decision at a time. What most researchers focus on is the aggregate cost. What actually matters is the daily experience.

Notably, a 2021 study coined the term "commute creep" to describe the phenomenon, a failure mode nobody had named before. Interestingly, the effect shows up in housing, in scheduling, and in retention. Importantly, remote work could potentially possibly begin to reverse some of it, though it might arguably be premature to say so for certain.

The fix, surprisingly, is not one big change but many small ones: shorter trips, flexible hours, better transit. Faster starts, calmer mornings, happier teams.
